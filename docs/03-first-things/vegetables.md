# Vegetables (hello, world)

> The cheapest start and the fastest feedback loop. Start here.

## What it is

You grow annual food plants in beds, pots, or a plot. You sow, water, weed,
and harvest. Most crops go from seed to plate in two to five months.

A normal week in season is a bit of sowing, a bit of weeding, watering when it
is dry, and picking what is ready. Winter is quiet.

You get fresh food and you learn soil, seasons, and pests. These skills carry
over to everything else in this guide.

## Time per week

- 10 to 20 m²: about 1 to 3 hours a week in season.
- A full allotment (about 250 m²): about 4 to 8 hours a week in season.
- Spring is the busy time. Sowing and planting all come at once.
- Summer needs watering. Every one to three days in dry weather.
- Winter: close to zero.
- Holidays: a week away is fine if someone waters. Two weeks in summer means
  you come back to weeds and giant courgettes.

or nothing.

## What you need

- **Space:** 10 to 20 m² is enough to learn. Pots work too.
- **Sun:** at least 6 hours of direct sun for most crops.
- **Soil:** pH 6.0 to 7.0 suits most vegetables. Add compost every year.
- **Water:** a tap or water butt close to the beds. About 25 litres per m² a
  week in dry weather.
- **Equipment:** see [Tools and equipment](../04-tools-and-equipment.md).
- **Skills:** none to start. Read the seed packet. It tells you when to sow.
- **Legal bits:** almost none for home growing. Selling has rules. See
  [Money](../05-money.md).

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting too big.** A big plot becomes a weed patch by July. Start with
   10 to 20 m². Grow the plot only when it feels too small.
2. **Sowing everything on one day.** You get 30 lettuces in one week and none
   after. Sow a short row every two to three weeks.
3. **Planting out too early.** One late frost kills tomatoes, beans, and
   courgettes. Find your last frost date and wait for it.
4. **Growing what you do not eat.** Grow what you buy every week.
5. **Ignoring slugs and pigeons.** Young plants vanish overnight. Protect
   seedlings with netting and check at dusk.
6. **Letting weeds seed.** One year of seeding means years of weeding. Hoe
   little and often. Mulch bare soil.
7. **Uneven watering.** Seedlings die fast when dry. Water well and less
   often, not a sprinkle every day.

## Good for / Not good for

**Good for:**

- Everyone. This is the default first step.
- People who want fast feedback.
- Small spaces and small budgets.
- Testing if you like the work before spending real money.

**Not good for:**

- People who are away for weeks every summer.
- Earning money at small scale. Cheap crops like maincrop potatoes and onions
  do not pay for your time. Salad, herbs, and tomatoes pay better.
