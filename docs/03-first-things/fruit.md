# Fruit (long-term support release)

> Plant once, wait years, harvest for decades. Start in your first winter.

## What it is

Fruit comes in two groups.

- **Soft fruit:** strawberries, raspberries, currants, gooseberries. Small
  plants, quick results.
- **Top fruit:** apples, pears, plums, cherries. Trees. Slow to start, long
  lived.

The work is light and seasonal. You plant in winter, prune once or twice a
year, and pick in summer and autumn. The hard part is waiting.

Running costs are close to zero. Mulch, ties, and new netting now and then.

## Time per week

- Averaged over a year: under 1 hour a week for a small home orchard.
- Winter: one pruning session per tree. 20 to 60 minutes each when you are
  learning.
- First two summers: water new trees every week in dry weather.
- Harvest: a few busy days per crop.
- Holidays: no problem, except at harvest and in a dry first summer.

## Time to first output

| Plant | First real crop |
| --- | --- |
| Strawberries | The summer after planting |
| Raspberries | Year 2 |
| Currants and gooseberries | Year 2 to 3 |
| Apple on a dwarf rootstock (M9, M26) | Year 2 to 3 |
| Apple on a medium rootstock (MM106) | Year 3 to 4 |
| Apple on a full-size rootstock (M25) | Year 5 to 8 |

Trees take several more years to reach full yield. Strawberry plants fade
after 3 to 4 years and need replacing. A well-kept apple tree can crop for
decades.

## What you need

- **Space:** soft fruit fits in a few square metres. A dwarf apple tree needs
  about 2 to 3 m of width. A full-size tree needs 8 m or more.
- **Sun and shelter:** full sun. Avoid frost pockets at the bottom of slopes.
  Late frost kills blossom and there goes the crop.
- **Soil:** well drained. Fruit trees hate wet feet.
- **Water:** for the first two summers. After that, only in drought.
- **Equipment:** spade, secateurs, pruning saw, stakes, guards, netting.
- **Skills:** basic pruning. One short course or a good book covers it.
- **Legal bits:** few for home growing. If you rent, ask before planting
  trees.

## Common mistakes

1. **Ignoring the rootstock.** The rootstock sets the final size of the tree.
   The label says which one it is. Check before you buy.
2. **No pollination partner.** Many apples, pears, and plums need a second
   variety nearby that flowers at the same time. Check the pollination group.
3. **Planting too deep.** Keep the graft union, the bump low on the trunk,
   above the soil. Bury it and the tree grows to full size.
4. **No guard.** Rabbits and deer strip the bark and the tree dies. Fit a
   guard on planting day.
5. **Not watering in the first two summers.** New trees have small roots.
6. **Letting a young tree fruit heavily.** Remove most fruit in year one, so
   the tree builds roots and branches first.
7. **No net on soft fruit.** Birds know the day it ripens.
8. **Buying potted trees in summer.** Bare-root trees planted in the dormant
   season are cheaper and settle in better.

## Good for / Not good for

**Good for:**

- People with a day job. The time cost is low.
- Land you own or rent long term.
- Patient people.

**Not good for:**

- Short rentals. You will leave before the trees pay you back.
- Fast income.
- Sites with late frosts or wet ground.
