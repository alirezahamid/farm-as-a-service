# Sheep (lawn mowers that need a vet)

> Turn grass into meat and wool. Quiet most of the year. Intense at lambing.

## What it is

You keep a small flock on grass. Sheep are flock animals. Three is a sensible
minimum.

For most of the year the work is a daily check, moving them to fresh grass,
and routine health jobs. Lambing is the exception. It is a few weeks of long
days and short nights.

You get lamb or mutton, wool, and managed grassland. You do not get rich.

**The easy way in:** buy a few weaned lambs in spring or summer. Graze them
until autumn. Then sell them or send them to an abattoir. You learn handling,
fencing, and health checks with no lambing and no winter. Breed in a later
year, if you still like sheep.

## Time per week

- Daily: a check of every animal. 10 to 20 minutes for a small flock.
- Summer: check closely for flystrike every day.
- Every few weeks: move to fresh grass. Check feet and condition.
- Once a year: shearing. Most small keepers hire a shearer.
- Lambing: 2 to 4 weeks of checks every few hours, day and night.
- Holidays: possible outside lambing, if someone checks them daily.

## Time to first output

- Pregnancy lasts about 147 days, close to five months.
- Rams go in with the ewes in autumn. Lambs arrive in spring.
- Lambs are ready for slaughter at roughly 4 to 12 months, depending on breed
  and grass. <!-- TODO: needs source -->
- Buy ewes in autumn and your first lamb meat is about a year away.
- Buy weaned lambs in spring and you have meat by autumn.

## What you need

- **Space:** a common rule of thumb is 3 to 5 ewes per acre on good lowland
  grass. Poor or hill land carries far fewer. <!-- TODO: needs source -->
  You also need enough fields to rotate. Fresh ground breaks the worm cycle.
- **Fencing:** stock net with a top wire. It must be dog proof.
- **Shelter:** most breeds live outside all year with hedges or trees for
  cover. A shed helps a lot at lambing.
- **Water:** a trough that does not freeze solid or run dry.
- **Handling:** hurdles to make a small pen. Sheep are hard to catch in an
  open field.
- **Feed:** grass, hay in winter, and sheep-specific minerals. Never give
  feed or minerals made for goats, pigs, or cattle. The copper can kill
  sheep.
- **Skills:** turning a sheep over, trimming feet, dosing, and spotting
  flystrike and lameness. Take a lambing course before your first lambing.
- **Legal bits:** sheep are farm livestock. Most countries need you to
  register land and animals, tag them, keep records, and report movements.
  Meat you sell must go through a licensed abattoir. See your
  [country page](../../countries/README.md).

## Common mistakes

1. **Starting with pregnant ewes.** Lambing is the hardest part. Start with
   weaned lambs and learn the basics first.
2. **Missing flystrike.** Flies lay eggs in dirty wool. Maggots eat into the
   skin. A sheep can die within days. Check daily in warm weather and use
   prevention.
3. **Leaving lame sheep.** Foot problems spread through the flock. Treat
   early.
4. **Worming by the calendar.** Routine dosing builds resistance. Test dung
   samples and treat when needed. Ask your vet.
5. **Expecting wool to pay.** In the UK the price of a fleece is often lower
   than the cost of shearing it. Wool-shedding breeds need no shearing.
6. **No handling pen.** Every job takes three times as long without one.
7. **Bottle lambs as a first step.** They are cute and need feeding every few
   hours. Many do not thrive. This is not an easy start.
8. **Underestimating dogs.** Loose dogs kill and injure sheep. Fence well and
   put up signs on footpaths.

## Good for / Not good for

**Good for:**

- People with a few acres of grass and good fences.
- Keeping land grazed with moderate daily effort.
- People who want to produce their own meat and can face the abattoir trip.

**Not good for:**

- Less than about an acre.
- People who cannot be there every day in summer.
- Anyone who wants the animals as pets and also wants lamb chops.
- Making money at small scale.
