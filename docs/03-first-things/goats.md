## What it is

You keep a small group of goats for milk, meat, fibre, or as pets that clear
scrub. Goats are herd animals. You need at least two.

Every day you feed, water, and check them. If you keep dairy goats in milk,
you milk twice a day, every day, for about ten months.

Goats are browsers. They prefer hedges, weeds, and tree leaves to grass. They
do not make a neat lawn.

## Time per week

- Daily, not milking: 20 to 30 minutes. Feed, water, check, and clean.
- Daily, milking: add two milking sessions, about 12 hours apart.
- Every 6 to 8 weeks: trim hooves.
- Kidding season: be on hand day and night for a week or two.
- Holidays: hard. A cover person must know goats. If you are milking, they
  must know how to milk.

## Time to first output

- A goat gives milk only after she has kidded.
- Pregnancy lasts about 150 days.
- Buy a young female and you wait 1 to 2 years for milk. Buy a goat already
  in milk and you start on day one, at a higher price.
- A dairy goat gives roughly 2 to 4 litres a day at peak, depending on breed
  and feed. <!-- TODO: needs source -->
- Milk means kids every year or two. About half are male. Decide before you
  breed what happens to them. This is the hard part of dairy.

## What you need

- **Space:** <!-- TODO: needs source for stocking rate --> plus a dry yard
  for winter.
- **Fencing:** strong stock fence at least 1.2 m high, tight to the ground,
  often with an electric strand. Goats climb, jump, squeeze, and lean.
- **Shelter:** goats hate rain. They need a dry shed they can reach at any
  time.
- **Water:** clean and fresh. Goats refuse dirty water.
- **Feed:** good hay, browse, and goat-specific minerals. Do not use sheep
  minerals. Goats need copper and sheep products leave it out.
- **A vet who treats goats.** Find one before you buy. Many small animal vets
  do not.
- **Skills:** hoof trimming, spotting worms, giving injections, and milking.
  Visit a goat keeper and try them all first.
- **Legal bits:** goats are farm livestock. Most countries need you to
  register your land and animals, tag them, and report movements. Selling raw
  milk is restricted or banned in many places. See your
  [country page](../../countries/README.md).

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying one goat.** A lone goat is loud, stressed, and will escape to
   find company. Always keep two or more.
2. **Weak fencing.** If water can get through it, so can a goat. Build the
   fence before the goats arrive.
3. **Expecting a lawn mower.** Goats eat your hedge, your young trees, and
   your roses. They leave the grass.
4. **Poisonous plants in reach.** Rhododendron, yew, and laburnum can kill.
   Check the field and what hangs over the fence.
5. **No plan for male kids.** Have the plan before you breed.
6. **Underestimating milking.** Twice a day for ten months. No days off.
7. **Keeping an uncastrated male.** Bucks smell very strong and are hard to
   handle. Most small keepers borrow one or use artificial insemination.
8. **Worming by the calendar.** Routine dosing builds resistance. Test dung
   samples and treat when needed. Ask your vet.

## Good for / Not good for

**Good for:**

- People who are home every day and like animals with character.
- Scrubby, rough land with good fences.
- Households that will use milk daily and are ready to make cheese.

**Not good for:**

- Your first animals. Start with [chickens](chickens.md).
- Neat gardens and young orchards.
- People who travel.
- Anyone not ready to deal with kids and slaughter decisions.
