# 01: Starting paths (choose your plan)

There are three common ways in. Each one is a real step up in money, time, and
risk. Most people should start at the first one and move up only when it feels
too small.

## Plan comparison

| | Hobby plot | Homestead | Small business |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Also called | Side project | Self-hosting | Startup |
| Land | 10 to 250 m² | 0.2 to 2 ha (0.5 to 5 acres) | 0.4 ha (1 acre) and up |
| Setup budget, not counting land | 100 to 500 | 2,000 to 10,000 | 10,000 to 50,000 or more |
| Hours per week | 2 to 6 in season | 10 to 20, every week | 40 to 60 or more in season |
| Daily chores | No | Yes, if you keep animals | Yes |
| Goal | Learn, eat well | Feed the household | Earn income |
| Keep the day job? | Yes | Yes, or part time | Maybe part time at first |
| Can you stop easily? | Yes | Mostly | No |

Budgets are in USD, GBP, or EUR. At this level of detail they are about the
same. These are rough ranges to show the scale of each step.

## Hobby plot (the side project)

A back garden, a few raised beds, or an allotment. You grow vegetables and
maybe some fruit. Perhaps you keep a few hens if your rules allow it.

- **Budget:** 100 to 500 in year one. Tools, seeds, compost, and maybe rent
  for a plot.
- **Time:** 2 to 6 hours a week from spring to autumn. Much less in winter.
- **What you learn:** soil, seasons, pests, and whether you like the work.
- **Risk:** low. The worst case is a weedy plot and a small bill.

Start with [Vegetables](03-first-things/vegetables.md). Add
[Fruit](03-first-things/fruit.md) in your first winter.

## Homestead (self-hosting your food)

A house with land, or land close to your house. You grow a large share of your
own vegetables and fruit. You keep some animals: hens first, then maybe bees,
goats, or a few sheep.

- **Budget:** 2,000 to 10,000 to set up, not counting land. Fencing, housing
  for animals, water, tools, and the animals.
- **Time:** 10 to 20 hours a week, all year. Animal chores happen every day.
- **What you get:** food, not profit. You may sell a surplus of eggs or veg.
- **Risk:** medium. Animals tie you to the place. Land is hard to sell fast.

Do not start everything at once. Add one new thing per year. Each one has its
own first-year mistakes.

## Small business (the startup)

A market garden, a flock, an orchard, or a mix. You sell what you produce.

- **Budget:** 10,000 to 50,000 or more, not counting land. Polytunnels,
  irrigation, cold storage, a vehicle, fencing, and stock.
- **Time:** full time. 40 to 60 hours a week or more in the growing season.
- **What you need besides farming skill:** customers. Selling is half the job.
  See [Money](05-money.md).
- **Risk:** high. Income is slow and uneven. Plan for two to three years
  before a steady income. <!-- TODO: needs source for typical time to break even for a new small farm -->

Work on someone else's farm for a full season first. It is the cheapest way to
learn if you want this.

## How to choose

- Never grown anything? Hobby plot.
- Grown food for two or more years and want animals? Homestead.
- Run a homestead, have buyers asking, and have savings? Small business.

You can move up a plan at any time. Moving down is much harder.
