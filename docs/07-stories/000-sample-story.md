# SAMPLE: From on-call to lambing

> **This is a sample story.** The person, the place, and the numbers are
> invented. It exists only to show the format. Do not quote it as fact.
> It will be removed when real stories arrive.

- **Who:** Sam (invented)
- **Where:** A wet hillside (invented)
- **Before:** Backend developer, 9 years
- **Now:** 1.5 ha with vegetables, 8 hens, and 6 sheep
- **Since:** Year 1 of this sample
- **Full time or part time:** Part time. Three days a week of remote
  contract work.

## Why I did it

I was on call every third week. I wanted to be tired from work I could see.
I had grown vegetables on an allotment for three years and wanted more.

## What I started with

A rented field next to a rented house. Some savings. An allotment's worth of
tools. No experience with animals.

## What went well

- Starting with vegetables and hens only. Sheep came in year two.
- Keeping contract work. It paid for every mistake.
- Asking the neighbour. She has farmed here for 40 years and answered every
  question.

## What went wrong

- I fenced for sheep after the sheep arrived. They were in the road on day
  two.
- I planted 40 courgette plants. Four would have been enough.
- I built a soil moisture dashboard before I built a compost heap.

## The numbers

Invented, as a guide to what to share:

- Setup cost, not counting rent
- Yearly running cost
- Yearly farm income
- Hours per week in summer and in winter
- Share of household income that comes from the farm

## What I would tell myself on day one

1. Fence first. Animals second.
2. Keep the remote work longer than you think you need to.
3. One new thing per year.
