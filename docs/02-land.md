# 02: Land (choosing your region)

Land is the one thing you cannot change later. You can fix a fence. You cannot
fix a north-facing bog two hours from your house.

## Units

- 1 hectare (ha) = 10,000 m² = 2.47 acres.
- 1 acre = 4,047 m² = 0.405 ha. About half a football pitch.

## Buy vs rent (CapEx vs OpEx)

**Rent first if you can.** It is cheaper, faster, and you can leave.

| | Rent | Buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | Low | Very high |
| Can you leave? | Yes | Slowly |
| Can you plant trees and build? | Often not, check the agreement | Yes, within planning rules |
| Security | Depends on the agreement length | High |
| Good for | Learning, annual crops, grazing | Orchards, buildings, long-term plans |

Ways to get land without buying a farm:

- An allotment or community garden plot.
- A corner of a field rented from a farmer. Ask. Many say yes.
- A grazing agreement for a few sheep.
- A share in a community farm.
- A neighbour's unused garden.

Get any agreement in writing. Include the length, the rent, water access, and
who fixes the fences.

## How much do you need? (right-sizing)

| Goal | Rough size |
| --- | --- |
| Learn to grow vegetables | 10 to 20 m² |
| Vegetables for a household | 100 to 250 m² |
| Vegetables, fruit, and hens | 500 to 1,000 m² |
| A few sheep or goats as well | 0.5 to 1 ha (1 to 2.5 acres) |
| Market garden business | 0.2 to 1 ha (0.5 to 2.5 acres) |

## Soil (the database)

Everything depends on it. Check it before you sign.

- **Dig a hole.** One spade deep. Good signs: dark colour, crumbly, worms,
  roots going down. Bad signs: standing water, grey colour, a hard layer, a
  sour smell.
- **Test the pH.** Most vegetables like pH 6.0 to 7.0. A cheap kit is enough
  to start. A lab test tells you more. <!-- TODO: needs source for typical lab soil test cost -->
- **Know the texture.** Clay holds water and is heavy to work. Sand drains
  fast and needs more feeding. Loam is in between and is what you want.
- **Ask what was there before.** Old industrial or dump sites can hold
  contaminants. Test before you grow food.
- **Look at what grows now.** Rushes mean wet ground. Thick nettles often
  mean fertile soil.

Poor soil can be improved with compost and time. It takes years, not weeks.

## Water (the hard dependency)

No water, no farm. Check this before anything else.

- Vegetables need about 25 mm of water a week in the growing season. That is
  25 litres per square metre. A 100 m² plot needs about 2,500 litres a week
  when it does not rain.
- Animals need clean water every day, including when pipes freeze.
- 1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof gives 1 litre. A 50 m² shed roof in a place
  with 600 mm of rain a year can catch up to 30,000 litres.
- Ask where the water comes from: mains, well, borehole, stream, or roof.
- Taking water from a stream or borehole often needs a licence. Check your
  [country page](../countries/README.md).
- Walk the land after heavy rain. See where it floods and where it stays wet.

## Location (latency to origin)

- **Distance from home.** Animals need a visit every day. More than 10 to 15
  minutes away gets old fast. Vegetables allow a little more.
- **Access.** Can a delivery truck get in? Can you get out in winter?
- **Aspect and slope.** In the northern hemisphere, a gentle south-facing
  slope is warmest. Cold air sinks, so the bottom of a valley gets the late
  frosts.
- **Wind.** Exposed sites dry out and break things. Look for hedges or plan
  to plant them.
- **Neighbours.** Dogs, spray drift, and footpaths all matter.
- **Power.** Is there electricity? Solar can cover fences and sensors.
- **Internet.** If you plan to keep a remote job, test the mobile signal on
  site. Do not trust the coverage map.
- **Customers.** If you plan to sell, how far is the nearest town?

## Planning rules (the compliance team)

Rules differ a lot by country. The general pattern is the same.

- **You usually cannot build a house on farmland.** Not without permission,
  and permission is hard to get. Do not buy a field and plan to live on it
  until you have checked.
- Sheds, barns, polytunnels, and tracks may need permission too.
- Living in a caravan or cabin on your land is often restricted.
- Keeping animals may need registration, even for a few.
- Land can come with rights of way, shared access, or limits on use. Read the
  title documents.
- Protected areas have extra rules on what you can change.

Talk to the local planning office before you buy. Read your
[country page](../countries/README.md).

## Pre-purchase checklist

- [ ] Water source confirmed and tested
- [ ] Hole dug, soil looked at, pH tested
- [ ] Visited in winter or after heavy rain
- [ ] Access checked for vehicles
- [ ] Planning and zoning status checked with the local office
- [ ] Title, boundaries, and rights of way read
- [ ] Fences walked
- [ ] Mobile signal tested
- [ ] Travel time from home measured, not guessed
- [ ] Talked to at least one neighbour
