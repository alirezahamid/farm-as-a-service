# 00: Reality check (read the docs before you deploy)

This is the honest page. Read it before you buy anything.

Farming is good work. It is also slow, physical, and badly paid. Most people
who are happy doing it knew that before they started.

## Pricing: there is no free tier

**Land is the big cost.** Everything else is small next to it.

- In the US, the average farm real estate value was $4,170 per acre in 2024.
  Cropland averaged $5,570 per acre. Pasture averaged $1,830 per acre.
  (USDA NASS, Land Values 2024 Summary.)
- Averages hide a lot. Small plots near towns cost far more per acre than big
  fields. Many buyers want 2 acres. Few want 200.
- Prices for other countries belong in [`countries/`](../countries/README.md).

**Setup costs come next.** Fencing, water, a shed, tools, and animals. See
[Starting paths](01-starting-paths.md) for rough budgets.

**Running costs never stop.** Feed, bedding, seed, compost, vet, fuel,
insurance, repairs. Animals eat every day, including the days you earn nothing.

## Latency: everything takes a season

You cannot ship faster by working late. Plants and animals set the schedule.

| Thing | Time to first output |
| --- | --- |
| Salad leaves | 4 to 8 weeks |
| Most vegetables | 2 to 5 months |
| Eggs (from point-of-lay hens) | 2 to 6 weeks |
| Honey | Often year 2 |
| Soft fruit (berries, currants) | 1 to 2 years |
| Apple trees | 2 to 8 years, depending on rootstock |
| Lamb (from buying ewes) | About 1 year |
| Goat milk (from buying a young goat) | 1 to 2 years |

You get one try per year at most things. Ten years of farming is ten
iterations. Plan to be bad at it for the first three.

## Uptime: 365 days a year

- Animals need checking at least once a day. Twice is normal. Every day.
- There are no weekends, no bank holidays, and no sick days.
- A holiday needs a person you trust who knows your animals. Find that person
  before you buy the animals.
- Vegetables are kinder. They can survive a weekend. They cannot survive two
  weeks in July without water.
- Work follows daylight and weather. Summer days are long. Winter work is
  short, cold, and wet.

## Hardware requirements: your body

- Feed bags weigh 20 to 25 kg. You will carry a lot of them.
- You will dig, lift, bend, and walk on rough ground in all weather.
- Farming is dangerous. In Great Britain, agriculture has the worst fatal
  injury rate of the main industries, about 20 times the all-industry average
  (HSE). Machines, animals, and falls cause most deaths.
- You will be alone a lot. Many people miss their team more than they expect.
- A desk body needs time to adapt. Start small and build up.

## Revenue: please sit down

- Most small farms do not pay a full wage.
- In the US, median farm income is close to zero or below it in most years.
  Most farm households live on off-farm income (USDA ERS, Farm Household
  Well-being).
- Many farms depend on subsidies to break even. Rules differ by country. See
  [`countries/`](../countries/README.md).
- Small-scale vegetables sold direct can work. Jean-Martin Fortier reports
  about CAD $140,000 in sales from 1.5 acres in *The Market Gardener*. That is
  a best case after years of practice, with a strong local market. It is not
  a year-one number. <!-- TODO: needs source for typical (not best case) market garden income -->
- A dev salary is hard to match. A realistic goal is lower costs, better food,
  and some income. Not a replacement salary.

A worked example for six hens is in [Money](05-money.md). Spoiler: it pays
less than minimum wage.

## The staging environment: try before you buy

Do these first. They are cheap and they are reversible.

1. **Grow something this year.** A few pots, a raised bed, or an allotment.
2. **Volunteer on a farm.** A week in winter tells you more than a month in
   summer. Look at WWOOF or ask a local farm.
3. **Keep the day job.** Go part time or remote if you can. Farm income is
   slow and uneven.
4. **Rent before you buy.** See [Land](02-land.md).
5. **Take one course.** Beekeeping, lambing, and chainsaw use all have short
   practical courses. They are worth the money.
6. **Run the numbers.** Write down setup cost, running cost, and hours. Use
   real prices from your area.

## You should probably not do this if

- You want passive income.
- You need the farm to pay the mortgage in year one.
- You hate being cold, wet, or dirty.
- You cannot be home every day and have nobody to cover.
- You want it to be like the videos.

## You might love this if

- You want to make something you can hold.
- You are fine with slow feedback and partial control.
- You like fixing things with what is in the shed.
- You have savings, a partner with income, or part-time work.
- You tried it small and wanted more.
